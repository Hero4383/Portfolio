Agent Systems Simulator

An interactive architecture metaphor for bounded agent roles, staged handoffs, validation, and recovery. This is a public simulation—not the TreeOfLife control plane.

What the simulation explores

The animation turns orchestration concepts into something visible and approachable.

  • Goals move from a coordinator to specialists and narrowly bounded workers.
  • Each work cell has a defined purpose and limited surface.
  • Handoffs carry results and evidence to the next stage.
  • Validation, cleanup, and recovery are represented as first-class steps.

Design ideas represented

Explicit boundaries

Work is divided into small cells with a clear objective and constrained authority.

Specialized roles

Coordinators, specialists, and workers contribute at different levels of scope.

Evidence at handoff

Progress is represented as a chain of receipts instead of an unverified final answer.

Failure as a state

Retries, cleanup, and recovery paths remain visible in the simulated workflow.

Conceptual loop

The public demo intentionally abstracts implementation and infrastructure details.

How to read the animation

Every visual element stands in for a systems concept rather than a literal deployment diagram.

  • Nested boxes represent increasingly narrow work boundaries.
  • Moving agents represent role-specific execution.
  • Pipes represent scoped requests and returned evidence.
  • Status stages represent tool use, validation, persistence, and completion.

What the working system has demonstrated

  • Role-bounded orchestration with durable operational state.
  • Disposable candidate workspaces and validation-gated delivery.
  • Local-model inference through brokered execution.
  • Clean-host restoration from a verified encrypted checkpoint.

What stays private

  • Model weights, prompt strategies, and proprietary datasets.
  • Internal service topology and provider credentials.
  • Deployment secrets and contract-specific automation.

Why it matters

Autonomy becomes more useful when scope, evidence, failure, and recovery are visible parts of the design.
