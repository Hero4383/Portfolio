TreeOfLife — Governed Autonomous Engineering

✓ Recovery verified

TreeOfLife turns complex goals into bounded, auditable work. Specialized roles plan, implement, validate, and operate through explicit contracts. Model and tool access is brokered by policy, and software changes stay isolated until the exact revision passes its required checks.

The platform brings together durable operational state, provenance-aware knowledge, role-scoped context, operator visibility, local-model support, and recovery tooling.

Latest verified milestone

A clean cloud host was rebuilt from an encrypted off-host checkpoint. Restored code, durable state, core services, operator views, and local inference were then verified end to end.

Engineering approach

Autonomy should be earned through evidence.

I build systems to make progress without surrendering boundaries, traceability, or recovery.

  1. 01

    Plan with constraints

    Define the objective, allowed surface, risk, validation, and rollback before execution.

  2. 02

    Implement in isolation

    Keep experimental work away from trusted state until it has produced reviewable evidence.

  3. 03

    Validate exact results

    Promote the revision that passed—not a look-alike—and connect completion to deployed health.

  4. 04

    Recover deliberately

    Treat checkpoints and restoration rehearsals as part of the product, not an afterthought.

About

Systems thinking, grounded in stewardship.

I’m Nathan Storms, a software engineer focused on the layers that make AI useful beyond a single prompt: orchestration, context, policy, validation, operations, and recovery.

I care about explicit contracts, reproducible runs, measured health, and reversible changes. I prefer systems that fail clearly, preserve provenance, and earn greater autonomy through proof.

My Christian faith shapes that work as stewardship. I believe powerful technology should remain purposeful, accountable, and directed toward serving people well.
